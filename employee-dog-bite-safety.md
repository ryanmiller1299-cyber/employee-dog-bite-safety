## Why Employee Dog Bite Safety Matters

Dogs can become protective of their homes, owners, food, toys, or territory. Even a dog that normally behaves well may react unexpectedly when an unfamiliar person enters its space.

For employees working in the field, workplace dog bite prevention should therefore be treated as an important occupational safety issue. Employees should know how to identify potential hazards before entering a property and understand when it is appropriate to stop or postpone a task.

A strong dog bite prevention policy can help employees make safer decisions while reducing unnecessary exposure to potentially dangerous situations.

## Common Workplace Dog Bite Risks

Employees can encounter dogs in many different situations. Common risks include:

- Dogs running loose in yards
- Unsecured gates
- Dogs escaping through open doors
- Animals approaching employees on sidewalks or driveways
- Dogs protecting their owners or territory
- Multiple dogs at one property
- Dogs hidden behind vehicles, fences, or landscaping
- Customers allowing dogs to approach employees

Understanding these situations is an important part of employee dog bite prevention.

## Understanding Dog Body Language

One of the most valuable elements of dog bite safety training is learning how to recognize canine body language.

Employees should be cautious when a dog displays behaviors such as intense barking, growling, snarling, stiff posture, raised fur, lunging, showing teeth, or persistent staring.

However, employees should not rely on one behavior alone to determine whether a dog is safe. Dogs can react differently depending on their environment and circumstances.

Learning basic dog body language can help workers recognize potential warning signs and maintain a safer distance.

## Never Assume a Dog Is Friendly

A dog may appear calm but can still become defensive when an unfamiliar person enters its territory. Employees should never assume that a dog is safe simply because the animal is small, quiet, or described as friendly by its owner.

For effective workplace dog bite prevention, employees should avoid unnecessary interaction with unfamiliar dogs. They should not attempt to pet, feed, call, or play with a dog while performing their work duties.

## Dog Bite Prevention for Delivery Drivers

Delivery driver dog safety is particularly important because delivery employees may visit numerous properties during a single shift.

Before approaching a property, drivers should observe their surroundings and look for visible animals or signs that a dog may be present.

Important dog bite prevention tips for delivery drivers include:

- Look around the property before entering.
- Listen for barking or movement behind doors and fences.
- Keep gates closed whenever possible.
- Avoid approaching loose or uncontrolled dogs.
- Do not attempt to interact with unfamiliar animals.
- Maintain a safe distance from dogs.
- Follow company procedures when a property presents an animal hazard.
- Report recurring dangerous-dog locations through the appropriate company system.

These practices can strengthen overall delivery driver safety.

## What to Do When a Dog Approaches

Knowing what to do when a dog approaches you is an important component of employee safety training.

Employees should remain calm and avoid sudden movements. They should not scream at the dog or run away, as running can encourage a dog to chase.

Instead, employees should try to remain still and avoid unnecessary eye contact. They should keep their hands controlled and avoid reaching toward the animal.

If possible, the employee can use a physical object or barrier between themselves and the dog while moving toward a safe location.

The objective of dog attack prevention is to avoid escalating the encounter and create distance whenever possible.

## What to Do After a Dog Bite

Despite proper dog bite prevention procedures, an incident can still occur. Employees should know their organization's procedures for reporting workplace injuries and seeking appropriate medical attention.

After a dog bite or other significant dog-related incident, the employee should notify the appropriate supervisor or safety representative as soon as practical and follow the company's established workplace injury reporting process.

The incident should be documented according to company policy so that appropriate follow-up and prevention measures can be considered.

## Lone Worker Dog Bite Safety

Lone worker safety is particularly important when employees work without colleagues nearby. A delivery driver or field technician may have limited immediate assistance if an aggressive dog approaches.

Companies should incorporate lone worker dog bite prevention into their broader safety programs. Employees should know how to communicate with supervisors, report hazards, request assistance, and respond to emergencies.

Communication procedures, emergency contacts, check-in systems, and clear escalation processes can support lone worker protection.

## Employee Dog Bite Safety Training

Regular dog bite safety training can help employees remain prepared. Training should cover:

- Recognizing potential dog hazards
- Understanding basic dog body language
- Safe approaches to residential properties
- Avoiding interaction with unfamiliar dogs
- Responding when a dog approaches
- Reporting dangerous animals
- Following emergency procedures
- Reporting workplace injuries
- Understanding company-specific safety policies

Training should be practical and easy for employees to apply during real-world situations.

## Creating a Workplace Dog Bite Prevention Policy

Employers can strengthen employee dog bite safety by establishing a clear workplace dog bite prevention policy.

A comprehensive policy may explain how employees should approach properties, what to do when a dog is loose, when employees should avoid entering a location, how animal-related hazards should be reported, and what steps employees should take following an incident.
